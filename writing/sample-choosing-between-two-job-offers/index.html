Sample: Choosing between two job offers

Salary is the easiest thing to compare and usually the least important difference between two offers early in a career.

Sandeep PawarChief Project Officer, GEMS Education India

Two offers feels like a good problem until you have to choose. The salary figures are easy to compare, so they tend to take over the decision. Early in a career, they are rarely the most important difference.

Compare what you will learn in the first two years

Ask each employer what someone in your role does in a normal week, and what they are doing two years later. The answers tell you more about your future than the job title does.

Compare the managers, not the brands

You will spend more time with your manager than with the company’s logo. If you can, ask to speak with the person you will report to. Notice whether they talk about developing people or only about delivering work.

Write down what would make you leave

For each offer, write the most likely reason you would want to leave within a year. Long hours, unclear role, no one to learn from. The offer with the reason you can live with is often the better one.

Then look at the money

Money matters, especially if you are supporting family or repaying a loan. Be honest about what you need, as opposed to what would be nice. If both offers cover what you need, the difference is worth less than it looks.

Whichever you choose, tell the other employer promptly and politely. Industries are smaller than they seem.
