Sample: Your first ninety days at work

The first three months set how people see you for a long time. Spend them learning how the place works, not proving how much you know.

Sandeep PawarChief Project Officer, GEMS Education India

Nobody expects much from you in your first week. By the end of your third month, people have a settled picture of you, and that picture is hard to change. The ninety days in between matter more than most new joiners realise.

Learn how work actually moves

Every organisation has an official process and a real one. The org chart tells you who approves things. It does not tell you who people ask before they ask the approver, or which meetings decide things and which only report on them.

Spend your first month watching for this. Notice who gets copied on important emails. Notice whose opinion changes a discussion. Write it down; you will forget.

Ask more questions than feels comfortable

New joiners worry that questions make them look slow. The opposite is true for the first few months. A question in week two is cheap. The same question in month six is expensive, because by then people assume you know.

A useful habit: keep a running list, and take it to your manager once a week instead of interrupting all day.

Deliver one small thing well

You do not need a big win in your first ninety days. You need one piece of work that was on time, complete and needed no chasing. That single experience is what your manager will remember when the next opportunity comes up.

Reliability is noticed before brilliance, and it is remembered longer.

Check in before the review, not at it

At around day sixty, ask your manager directly: what is going well, and what should I do differently in the next month? Most people wait for the formal review. By then the impression is already set.
