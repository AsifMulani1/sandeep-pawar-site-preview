Sample: How to ask your manager for feedback

“Any feedback for me?” almost always gets “You’re doing fine.” A narrower question gets an answer you can use.

Sandeep PawarChief Project Officer, GEMS Education India

Most people ask for feedback in a way that makes it easy to say nothing. “Any feedback for me?” is a polite question, and it gets a polite answer: “No, you’re doing fine.” You leave the conversation knowing nothing new.

Ask about one piece of work

Feedback is easier to give about something specific. Instead of asking about your performance in general, ask about the presentation you gave on Tuesday, or the report you sent last week.

Ask for one change, not a verdict

“How did I do?” invites a grade. “What is one thing I could have done better in that meeting?” invites advice. The second question gives your manager permission to be honest without it feeling like criticism.

  • “If you had written that email, what would you have changed?”
  • “Was there a point in the presentation where I lost the room?”
  • “What would have made that report more useful to you?”

Make it safe to answer

How you react the first time decides whether you hear the truth the second time. Thank the person, ask one follow-up question, and do not explain why you did it that way. You can think about whether you agree later.

Close the loop

A few weeks later, tell them what you changed. It shows the feedback was worth giving, and it makes them more likely to offer it next time without being asked.
